# Reflection — Kasten Director of Storage and Backup Operations (v2 review)

*Reviewer: Director of Storage and Backup Operations, ex-Backup Administrator. Read as the person who will be asked to sign off on rolling this course out to my team, staff it, and answer for the result.*

## What I (think I) now know

I now have a real mental model, not a marketing one. Kasten protects an *application* — namespaced objects, cluster-scoped dependencies, Helm release metadata, and volumes together — not a machine, and the module drills that distinction until it sticks. I know a snapshot is not a backup until it's exported, and the course repeats that warning at every layer (concept map, storage integration, policy design) rather than saying it once and hoping it lands. I know Kasten runs as a workload inside the cluster it protects, which means it inherits the cluster's fate and has to protect itself — Kasten Disaster Recovery exists for exactly that reason, and I could explain to my own leadership why "who backs up the backup system" has a concrete, named answer here. I know the four built-in RBAC roles by name and by scope (`k10-admin` cluster-wide, `k10-basic` per namespace, `k10-config-view` read-only, `k10-virtualmachines-admin`), and I know the product explicitly warns that Direct Access and Basic Authentication configure no RBAC at all — that's the kind of line I'd otherwise have to dig for.

## What actually landed

The material that would let me walk into a governance conversation today, unprompted, includes: the dashboard's four compliance states (Unmanaged / Non-Compliant / Compliant / Removed) as a direct analogue to reading job sessions each morning; the explicit warning that "functionally the same" (Starter vs. Enterprise) is not "the same" and a Starter deployment is not a supported production deployment; the immutable-backups callout for Kasten DR profiles, framed correctly as ransomware/insider-deletion resilience rather than a checkbox; the RBAC section's own caution that granting `create`/`update`/`delete` on RBAC resources lets a user escalate their own privileges — that is exactly the kind of thing I'd expect a director-level reviewer to have to surface myself, and the course got there first. The daily/weekly/monthly/quarterly operating rhythm in Module 04 and Module 05 is close to something I could hand my team as a runbook skeleton without much editing.

## What confused me / where I couldn't answer "how would we prove this?" or "who owns this?"

Three places stopped me cold enough that I'd want a straight answer before I'd sign anything:

1. **Who can delete a backup, and can I prove it?** The RBAC table tells me `k10-basic` can back up, restore, create and modify policies, view details, and cancel actions — and conspicuously never says whether it can *delete* a restore point or a policy. That is the first question my CISO will ask, verbatim, and this module leaves me guessing at the answer for the one role most of my team will actually hold.
2. **Is there a "who did this" audit trail, not just a "what happened" activity log?** Module 04's Prometheus layer and Module 05's activity table both tell me about actions, policies, durations, and outcomes — never about which authenticated person triggered a manual restore or a manual deletion. Under Direct Access mode there's no authentication configured at all, so there may be nothing to attribute even in principle. If I can't name which screen answers "who deleted this restore point," I can't stand behind this in an audit.
3. **What happens when the platform team and I disagree on timing?** Module 04 is honest that a Kubernetes upgrade can drop my Kasten release out of the supported matrix, and that sequencing the two calendars is now my problem in a way it never was when the backup server owned its own Windows host. It never says who has the authority to block a cluster upgrade that would break my backup platform's support status. That's an escalation path I'd need written down, not implied.

A smaller but related gap: KDR's passphrase guidance says it should be "retrievable by someone other than you" — good instinct, no named owner. And the self-service/multi-tenancy direction in Module 05 (letting application teams own their own policies) is presented as a career upgrade for me without ever saying whether the compliance rollup I report to my VP still comes from my team once that delegation happens.

## What I'd still need before approving the rollout

Before I'd put my name on this for my team: (1) an explicit statement of delete permissions per role, not just an inference from what's listed; (2) confirmation of whether Kasten (or the surrounding stack) records user identity against destructive actions, and if not, an explicit acknowledgment that Direct Access and Basic Auth modes forfeit that traceability entirely — not just "not advisable"; (3) a named escalation owner for cluster-upgrade-vs-Kasten-support-matrix conflicts, ideally captured in a change-calendar agreement with the platform team; (4) a decision, made before rollout rather than discovered after, on who owns compliance reporting once policy ownership starts moving to application teams. None of these require new product capability — they require the course (or the rollout runbook that sits beside it) to say the sentence out loud instead of leaving it to be assumed.
