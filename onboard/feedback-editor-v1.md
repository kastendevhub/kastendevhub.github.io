# Editor critique — v1, Veeam Kasten for Kubernetes Fundamentals (self-paced, VDP admin path)

**Reviewer:** Senior Technical Editor · **Artifact:** `index.html` + `Lesson01`–`Lesson05` (v1) · **Read:** all six files, in course order
**Rows:** 26 — 2 critical / 11 significant / 13 minor → `review-rows-editor.json`
**Verdict:** approve with edits. Nothing here is a rewrite; the two criticals are both in Lesson 02 and both are one-line fixes.

---

## What works

**The pedagogical spine is genuinely good, and it is the same spine in all five modules.** Every Kubernetes concept is introduced through the thing the learner already administers, and then — this is the part most courses skip — the analogy is explicitly retired. Lesson 02 states the contract out loud ("what it is, the closest thing you already administer, where that comparison breaks, what it means for backup") and then honors it concept by concept. Lesson 01's five-row concept map with a "Where the analogy breaks" note on every row is the strongest single component in the course, and repeating it as a scannable table underneath the interactive was the right call for a learner who will come back to it later.

**The snapshot-versus-export distinction is taught the way a dangerous idea should be taught.** It gets its own section in Lesson 01, a durability section in Lesson 02, a compare-card treatment in Lesson 03, and it opens Lesson 05. Four passes, four different angles, and the framing is consistently structural rather than operational — "a policy with the snapshot action alone will report success indefinitely… the check is structural, not operational". That is the sentence that will save someone's data, and it is well placed.

**Citation discipline is the best I have seen on a v1 in this pipeline.** Inline `[Src: Kasten Docs 9.0.2 p.N]` on essentially every product claim, including inside quiz option rationales, which is where citations usually go missing. Code and CLI strings are quoted rather than paraphrased, the `kasten-io` namespace and `kasten/k10` chart name are handled as identifiers with an explicit supplementary note in Lesson 01 explaining why the learner will keep seeing `k10` — that note is a good decision and pre-empts exactly the confusion the naming standard creates. No `[VERIFY]` markers anywhere; no marketing filler (I grepped the banned list — zero hits); no all-caps emphasis except one instance.

**Accessibility holds up.** Descriptive alt text on all eleven screenshots (long, specific, figures included), `aria-label` on every rail dot and copy button, `visually-hidden` table captions, `aria-live` on the interactive detail panels, no colour-only signalling in the compliance-state cards. The interactives are keyboard-operable and each carries an honest "nothing scored" label.

**The two runbook modules know what they are.** Lesson 03's readiness checklist with five blocking and three follow-up items, and Lesson 04's upgrade planner applying only the two documented rules, are both scoped so they cannot bluff — Lesson 03's "a preparation aid, not a supported readiness tool" disclaimer is the right instinct. Lesson 05's five-situation restore decision widget is the best assessment-adjacent component in the course, and its two-question heuristic ("is the local snapshot still trustworthy; does this have to land on top of the running application") is a genuinely useful thing to carry into a bad day.

## Main weaknesses

**1. Lesson 02 is on a different course.** It calls its unit a "Lesson" thirteen times — breadcrumb, title, hero tag, objectives, buttons, prose, cross-references, footer — while the landing page, all four sibling modules, and Lesson 01's own next-link say "Module". A learner clicks "Module 02" and lands on "Lesson 02". This is the parallel-authoring seam and it is the single most visible defect in the build (ED-v1-001). Its footer also drops the version stamp the other four carry.

**2. One cross-reference points at the wrong module.** Lesson 02 tells the learner that snapshot-plus-export "is a policy decision you will make in Lesson 04". Policy construction is Module 05; Module 04 has no policy-building content at all (ED-v1-002). This is the only broken forward reference in the course — I checked every other one, including the eleven internal "Section N" pointers, and they all resolve.

**3. Terminology drifts across the parallel-authored seams, in small ways that a technical reader will notice.** The same four-state compliance model is worded three ways from the same source page — "service levels are being met" (L02), "service-level agreements are being respected" (L04), "service-level objectives are being respected" (L05) — and SLA/SLO are not synonyms to this audience (ED-v1-007). The editions are named "Starter (also called Veeam Kasten Free)" in Lesson 01 and "Veeam Kasten Free… the default Starter edition" in Lesson 03 (ED-v1-006). `Pod` is a capitalised object in Lesson 02 Section 03 and a lowercase noun in Lesson 02 Section 08 and all of Lesson 03 (ED-v1-019). Four footer formats across five files; three duration-chip formats across four modules and none in Lesson 01 (ED-v1-017, ED-v1-026).

**4. US-English spelling has not been applied.** Twenty-five British forms across five of the six files — `organised`, `labour`, `behaviour`, `fulfils`, `catalogue`, `honour`, `recognise`, `parameterised`, `summarising`, `centre`, `Practise`, `backwards compatibility` — and the same words appear correctly spelled elsewhere in the same course (`organized`, `visualizes`, `optimize`, `standardize`), so the inconsistency is visible inside single lessons, not just across them. This one is mechanical but it is a brand-visible gate, so it is graded significant and needs a single sweep (ED-v1-004).

**5. Two knowledge checks miss a stated objective, and one item is duplicated across modules.** Lesson 01 states five objectives and assesses four: objective 4 (what deploys into the cluster, and the editions) gets nothing, while objective 5 gets two questions — and one of those two is a near-duplicate of Lesson 03's Q5, same premise, same answer, same source pages (ED-v1-009 fixes both with one retarget). Lesson 04 states six objectives and asks five questions, doubling up on objective 1 and assessing neither objective 2 nor objective 4; objective 4 ("collect the right diagnostics before you open a support case") is the one the learner will need under pressure and it has a clean five-step sequence to assess, so I have proposed a sixth item rather than deleting a good one (ED-v1-010, with ED-v1-011 for the counts that follow).

**6. Two naming residues.** Bare "Kasten" as the vendor name survives twice in Lesson 01 ("a license key from Kasten", "the Kasten product page") — the same legacy-branding problem the standard bans in its longer forms (ED-v1-005). And Lesson 02's caption asserts uppercase `K10` "appears inside the annotation key" when the key directly above it is lowercase `k10`, so the one place the course discusses the naming rule is the one place that breaks it (ED-v1-003). Lesson 03 also invents "Veeam Kasten Backup Server" in the Veeam Data Cloud Vault row, an object that exists nowhere else in the course and reads as a conflation of two different servers (ED-v1-014).

**7. Lesson 02 states no entry condition.** Lesson 01 and Lesson 03 both carry a prerequisites panel; Lesson 03's is explicit that Modules 01–02 supply the vocabulary it uses without redefining. Lesson 02 — the hinge the whole course leans on — states none, and it uses "the primer tool" with a definite article a full module before that tool is introduced (ED-v1-012, ED-v1-013). Modules 04 and 05 carry the chain in their hero blurbs, so Lesson 02 is the only real gap.

## On the course-level questions I was asked

- **Does the index describe each lesson accurately?** Yes, module for module, including the "Closes with the editions and where Veeam Backup & Replication integration fits" detail on card 01. Two small gaps: the card-02 description stops at Helm and does not mention Section 08 (where Veeam Kasten itself runs), and the "five-question knowledge check" promise becomes a lie in Module 04 if ED-v1-010 is accepted. The hero lede introduces the short product name before the long one (ED-v1-015).
- **Is the Helm install duplicated between Lessons 02 and 03?** No — each occurrence earns its place, and this is done well. Lesson 02 §07 uses the three commands to teach what a chart, a release and a values override *are*, labels the code block "shell · what Lesson 03 will run", and reads them back as three sentences. Lesson 03 §3 is the runbook: five-stage flow, platform-specific flags, AWS credential variants, pod verification, release-name consequences. Preview versus runbook, exactly as intended. Two notes: Lesson 02 includes `helm repo update` and Lesson 03's walkthrough does not, and the VolumeSnapshotClass YAML also appears twice (L02 abridged, L03 complete) — that pair is also defensible, since L02 is teaching recognition and L03 is teaching configuration.
- **Do the lessons honour the prerequisite chain?** Almost. Nothing is used before it is taught except "the primer tool" in Lesson 02 (ED-v1-012) and, marginally, Kanister blueprints — Lesson 01 introduces them lowercase in the concept map, Lesson 02 capitalises them as "Kanister Blueprints" and points forward to policy building, and Lesson 05 covers hooks in the scheduling stepper. That chain works; only the capitalisation drifts.
- **Are the VDP-parallel analogies consistent lesson to lesson?** Yes, and this is the course's real achievement. vCenter Server ↔ cluster API, ESXi host ↔ node, VM ↔ namespaced application, backup job ↔ policy, backup repository ↔ location profile, application-aware processing ↔ Kanister blueprints, proxies/agents ↔ in-cluster services, configuration backup ↔ Veeam Kasten Disaster Recovery, morning job-session read ↔ dashboard compliance states. Every one is introduced once, reused with the same mapping, and retired at the same boundary. I found no contradictory analogy across the five modules — which, for five parallel authors, is the result worth noting.

## Ship-readiness

**Approve with edits — clear the two criticals before anything else.** Both are in Lesson 02, both are single-line, and neither requires the author to reopen a section: the Lesson→Module sweep (ED-v1-001, thirteen instances listed) and the wrong forward reference (ED-v1-002). With those two applied, the build is learner-ready; the eleven significant rows are the difference between a good course and one that survives a brand review, and the US-English sweep (ED-v1-004) should be treated as a gate rather than a preference because it touches five files.

The thirteen minor rows are polish and can be batched or declined without harm, except ED-v1-011, which is only valid if ED-v1-010 is accepted — decline it in lockstep if the sixth question is declined.

What I could not judge: the technical accuracy of the cited claims. I read every `[Src: …]` for internal consistency, quotation fidelity and page-plausibility, and the citation density and specificity are strong, but I did not open the 9.0.2 PDF pages — that is the Architect's pass. Three places I would want a current-build SME to look at specifically: the Kubernetes/OpenShift support matrix in Lesson 04 §02 (six rows, all cited to p.862, and the kind of table that goes stale first), the "Veeam Kasten Backup Server" phrasing in Lesson 03 §6 (ED-v1-014), and Lesson 04's "releases every 2 weeks" cadence claim, which is the sort of statement that dates a course.
